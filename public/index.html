Know Before You Go

A few things that make the week smoother — and keep you on the island's good side.

  • Reef-safe sunscreen only. Hawaii bans sunscreens with oxybenzone and octinoxate — buy mineral (zinc). The reef and the law both thank you.
  • Give wildlife room. Never touch or crowd sea turtles (honu) or monk seals. It's protected by law.
  • Shoes off indoors. If you visit a local home, leave slippers at the door.
  • Carry some cash. Shrimp trucks, shave ice stands, and farmers markets are often cash-only.
  • Respect the ocean. Check surf and never turn your back on big waves. When in doubt, don't go out.
  • Book the big stuff early. Arizona Memorial, Hanauma Bay, Diamond Head, shark dives, and the PCC all sell out.
  • Pack: reef shoes, a light rain layer, a dry bag, and offline maps — parts of the North Shore have no signal.

Summer & Winter

Oʻahu is great year-round, but the ocean behaves very differently by season.

Summer

roughly May – Sept

  • North Shore goes calm — snorkel Sharks Cove & Three Tables, swim Waimea.
  • Try the Waimea Bay rock jump on flat days.
  • Best water clarity island-wide.
  • Electric Beach (west) for turtles and fish.
  • Long, hot days — hike at dawn.

Winter

roughly Nov – March

  • Humpback whale season — cruises, or spot them from Makapuʻu.
  • Big-wave season: Pipeline, Sunset, Waimea firing. Watch the pros.
  • Do not swim the North Shore when it's big.
  • Waterfalls run full; trails green and muddy.
  • Waikīkī stays swimmable.
